Book List · Direction D · Editorial calm

Let the titles do the talking.

No cards, no containers, no drop shadows — hairlines and whitespace do all the dividing, and the type scale does all the ranking. A prescribed booklist is a piece of typography before it is a form, so the titles are set at 19px in a serif and the running total at 44px, with one bottle green reserved for anything that commits. It serves a parent who wants to read the list carefully once rather than skim it, and a vendor whose screen should look like a well-set ledger — quietly expensive, which is exactly the "cut above the market" the brief asks for.

IV

One surface, two measures

NFR1 · NFR6 · desktop first-class

Editorial layout is really a decision about measure. On the phone the list is a single narrow column with the total set beneath it. On the laptop the measure is held short rather than allowed to run the full window, the quantity and line total step out into their own columns, and the total becomes a rail — the same page, re-set for a wider sheet.

Accessibility, stated not assumed

  • Ink #16181A on page #FCFCFA is 17.1:1; the standfirst grey #55595E is 6.8:1, which is why it can be this light and still clear AA at 11.5px.
  • White on green #1F5145 is 9.0:1 and green on the page is 8.7:1, so the single accent works as a button, as a tick, as a rule and as link text without a second tone.
  • The caution colour is a dark ochre #6F5411 at 6.9:1 rather than a bright amber. A pale caution tone is the standard way an editorial palette fails AA, so it is ruled out here on purpose.
  • Control boundaries all use #6E6B64 at 5.1:1. The #D8D6D0 hairline is typographic furniture and never outlines a control — that single rule is what lets the page be this quiet and still pass at 3:1.
  • Focus is a 2px green ring with a 3px offset, legible on the page, on the green wash and on the green button. The atlas checkbox in the hero is shown in its focused state.
  • Whitespace is put around the targets, not taken out of them. Checkboxes sit in 44px cells, stepper buttons are 44×44px, and primary buttons are 52px — the airiness comes from row padding, not from small controls.
  • Status is never hue alone: "Not yet called" is italic with an open circle, "Called 9.15 · no answer" is roman with a filled square and a time, "Price agreed" carries a tick.
  • Motion implication: slow and few. A 260ms crossfade as a row settles into its unticked state, a total that eases rather than snaps, no motion at all in the vendor's confirm path (NFR7). All suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion.
